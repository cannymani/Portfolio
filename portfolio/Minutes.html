Minutes: Fixing the Broken Demand Flow

Minutes is an AI-enabled Demand Management experience designed to transform a fragmented, meeting-heavy demand process into a more connected journey — helping teams capture context, structure discussions, record decisions, assign actions, and keep demands moving toward resolution.
The concept explores how AI can become an active participant in enterprise workflows rather than simply another interface layer.

Demand Management sits at the intersection of business ideas, product strategy, architecture, governance, and delivery. The challenge was not simply that the existing system had usability issues. The larger problem was that the demand journey was fragmented across people, meetings, tools, documents, approvals, and follow-up tasks.
Research revealed long approval cycles of 6–10 weeks, disconnected tools, weak meeting governance, limited visibility into demand status, growing backlog, and significant manual documentation effort.

For Demand Managers, Product Owners, and Solution Architects, this created a recurring pattern:
Prepare → Discuss → Document → Follow up → Chase → Revisit

Instead of:
Understand → Decide → Act → Track

The Challenge

The existing demand journey was designed around processes and handoffs rather than the conversations and decisions that actually shape a demand.

The opportunity was to rethink the experience around how people capture an idea, understand its value, discuss it with stakeholders, make decisions, and move it forward — while using AI to reduce the administrative effort involved in that journey.

My Role

I explored the problem from both a product strategy and UX perspective, mapping the demand lifecycle and identifying opportunities where AI could meaningfully assist users rather than simply add another AI layer.

I translated those opportunities into an end-to-end experience spanning demand capture, AI-assisted structuring, collaboration, evaluation, prioritization, and progression toward delivery.

Learning

  • Enterprise dashboards should answer decisions, not simply display data.
  • Information architecture becomes critical when users operate across hundreds of entities.
  • Governance works better when the experience makes the next action obvious.
  • Data visualisation should progressively reveal complexity rather than expose everything at once.
  • A scalable enterprise experience needs both consistency and flexibility.
  • UX leadership means creating systems and principles that continue to work beyond a single screen or release.
