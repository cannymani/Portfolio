ATIVA — Elevated AR Shopping Experience

A new AR-powered shopping experience that helps customers visualize products in their real environment and make more confident purchase decisions. ATIVA explores how augmented reality can reduce uncertainty in online shopping by allowing users to visualize, customize, and virtually try products before purchasing.
The original case study describes a 16-week project with 35+ screens, covering research, ideation, AR interaction design, prototyping, usability testing, and final UI design.

The Challenge

Online shopping often requires customers to make purchase decisions without being able to experience the product in their own environment. Product images and descriptions may not provide enough confidence about appearance, fit, size, colour or how the product will look in context.

This can lead to dissatisfaction, incorrect purchases and product returns.

My Role

I explored the problem from both a product strategy and UX perspective, starting with user research to understand how people make online and offline shopping decisions. Through interviews, affinity mapping, personas, mental models and journey mapping, I identified key moments where customers lacked confidence during product discovery and evaluation.

I translated these insights into an end-to-end AR shopping experience covering:

Discover → Scan → Visualize → Customize → Try On → Compare → Purchase

I designed and iterated the experience across three core AR capabilities:

01 — AR Spaces
Users can scan and saðe real-world spaces in their profile, allowing them to return later and visualize products in those environments.

02 — AR Scan
Users can scan an existing product, create a 3D representation and explore similar products available within the application.

03 — AR Try-on
Users can view a product in 3D, change angles, explore design/colour/size options and use AR to experience the product in their real environment.

Outcome

The project demonstrated how AR could move an online shopping experience beyond static product imagery toward a more immersive and confidence-driven purchase journey.

Created an end-to-end AR shopping experience covering discovery, visualization, customization, virtual try-on and purchase.
Identified key moments where AR could reduce uncertainty in the product evaluation journey.
Designed multiple AR interaction models, including AR Spaces, AR Scan and AR Try-on.
Established a framework for combining e-commerce interactions with spatial/AR experiences.
Validated the concept through usability evaluation and iterative prototyping.

Learning

  • Technology should solve a user problem, not become the experience.
    AR was most valuable when it addressed a specific uncertainty—“Will this look right in my environment?”—rather than simply providing an impressive interaction.
  • Confidence is more important than immersion.
    The real value of AR shopping is not just allowing users to interact with a 3D product. It is helping them make a more confident purchase decision.
  • Context makes visualization meaningful.
    Showing a product in the user's actual environment provides significantly more useful information than viewing the same product against a generic background.
  • Complex technology needs simple interactions.
  • The experience needs to connect back to the commerce journey.
    AR should not become a disconnected “wow” moment. The experience needs a clear path from:
    Exploration → Evaluation → Decision → Purchase.
  • Emerging technology requires designing for uncertainty.
